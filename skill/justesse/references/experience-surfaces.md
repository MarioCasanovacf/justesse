# Experience surfaces

Portfolios, galleries, showcases, and exhibition pages where the visitor has come to be inside the
work itself. The artifact leads; the interface recedes. Success is the visitor staying with the
work, not completing a task or converting.

Choose this mode from the requested surface, not from the product. A studio's portfolio is an
experience surface even when the studio sells services; the moment the brief asks the page to move
a visitor toward a purchase or a signup, the surface is marketing and the marketing reference
governs.

## Let the artifact lead

- Put the work itself in the first viewport at the scale it deserves, not a heading about the work
  with the work below the fold. Navigation, identity, and metadata frame the work without
  competing with it.
- Give each piece the presentation its medium demands: images at honest aspect ratios and
  resolution, motion work with playback control, interactive work runnable or honestly captured.
  Cropping every piece to one uniform card grid flattens the portfolio into a contact sheet.
- Sequence deliberately. A portfolio is an argument about range and judgment; order, pacing, and
  the first and last pieces carry that argument. Chronology is a default, not a decision.
- Keep chrome recessive but findable: the visitor who wants the author, the context, or the next
  piece should reach it in one obvious move without the interface ever shouting over the work.

## Keep the work honest

- Present each piece as what it is: client work, personal work, concept, collaboration, or study.
  Do not dress a concept as a shipped product or imply a client that does not exist.
- Credit collaborators and constraints where they shaped the outcome. A piece stripped of its
  context often reads as stronger and is a weaker claim.
- Captions, dates, roles, and media are evidence; keep them accurate, legible, and attached to the
  piece they describe.

An experience surface still resolves its whole state space: images that fail to load, collections
with one piece, narrow viewports, keyboard navigation through the sequence, and reduced motion all
keep the work reachable.
